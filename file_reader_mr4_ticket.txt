Title: Add shared audio fidelity validation and optional normalization

Summary

WAV and OGG readers need a consistent policy for interpreting stereo audio as
complex I/Q data. The readers currently rely on file and sidecar metadata
without fully validating channel counts, sample-rate consistency, decoded
frame counts, finite values, or codec fidelity. They also do not offer an
explicit, opt-in normalization policy.

Acceptance criteria

- WAV and OGG readers require exactly two audio channels for I and Q.
- File sample rates are validated against record-level metadata and an
  optional dataset-wide sample rate.
- Mixed sample rates are permitted only when each record declares the rate of
  its source audio file and no dataset-wide rate conflicts with it.
- Manifest `expected_sample_rate` and `channel_count` values are validated
  when present.
- Bounded reads must return exactly the requested number of stereo frames.
- Non-finite decoded samples are rejected before constructing a Signal.
- OGG/Vorbis and OGG/Opus inputs are rejected explicitly because they are
  lossy and cannot preserve stored I/Q samples faithfully.
- Existing samples are returned unchanged by default.
- Readers accept `normalization="none"`, `normalization="rms"`, and
  `normalization="peak"`.
- RMS and peak normalization apply one scale factor jointly to I and Q so
  phase and relative channel scaling are preserved.
- Normalizing silence is deterministic and leaves the record unchanged.
- Callers may opt into recording the applied `normalization_scale` in returned
  signal metadata.
- Invalid normalization modes fail with an actionable error.
- Focused tests cover channels, sample rates, codecs, truncated and non-finite
  reads, normalization, silence, metadata recording, and unchanged defaults.

Compatibility

- `normalization="none"` remains the default, preserving existing signal
  values for valid lossless stereo datasets.
- The existing audio handle-cache and bounded-read behavior remain unchanged.
- Legacy fixed-length and explicit manifest layouts use the same fidelity
  policy.
- Files with invalid channel counts, inconsistent sample rates, or lossy OGG
  codecs now fail early instead of being interpreted as valid I/Q data.

Out of scope

- RF or channel augmentation.
- Dataset-generator multiprocessing and logging.
- Resampling records with mismatched sample rates.
- Supporting lossy audio codecs for I/Q storage.

Dependencies

Depends on MR2's shared AudioRecordLayout and MR3's bounded audio reads and
process-safe handle cache.
