MR9 Unified HDF5 Reader Benchmark Findings
===========================================

Scope
-----

The representative MR9 benchmark completed 128 measurements across:

* legacy standard, packed, homogeneous, and structured HDF5 formats;
* complex IQ and floating-point spectrogram workloads;
* individual sequential, individual random, contiguous batch, and shuffled
  batch access;
* uncompressed and LZF-compressed storage; and
* chunk sizes of 1, 8, and 32 samples where the writer supports that control.

The run used 256 generated samples, 128 reads per measurement, a batch size of
32, one warmup, and three repetitions. Values, shapes, and dtypes were checked
for equivalence before timing. Steady-state results used a warm filesystem
cache.

Fastest configurations
----------------------

Complex IQ:

* Individual sequential: structured, LZF, chunk 1 at 44,885 samples/second.
* Individual random: structured, LZF, chunk 8 at 44,739 samples/second.
* Contiguous batch: homogeneous, uncompressed, chunk 8 at 324,980
  samples/second.
* Shuffled batch: structured, uncompressed, chunk 8 at 45,810 samples/second.

Spectrogram:

* Individual sequential: structured, uncompressed, chunk 8 at 175,899
  samples/second.
* Individual random: structured, uncompressed, chunk 1 at 177,255
  samples/second.
* Contiguous batch: homogeneous, uncompressed, chunk 32 at 221,884
  samples/second.
* Shuffled batch: structured, uncompressed, chunk 1 at 164,210 samples/second.

Shuffled format comparison
--------------------------

For shuffled IQ reads, each format's best result was:

* Standard: 1,862 samples/second.
* Packed: 7,576 samples/second.
* Homogeneous: 11,965 samples/second.
* Structured: 45,810 samples/second.

The best structured result was approximately 24.6x faster than standard, 6.0x
faster than packed, and 3.8x faster than homogeneous.

For shuffled spectrogram reads, each format's best result was:

* Standard: 2,183 samples/second.
* Packed: 8,288 samples/second.
* Homogeneous: 16,393 samples/second.
* Structured: 164,210 samples/second.

The best structured result was approximately 10x faster than homogeneous and
75x faster than standard.

Chunk-size findings
-------------------

Chunk size was especially important for shuffled access to large spectrogram
samples. Uncompressed structured throughput was:

* Chunk 1: 164,210 samples/second.
* Chunk 8: 43,435 samples/second.
* Chunk 32: 26,372 samples/second.

One-sample chunks prevent a random read from loading unrelated large samples.
For contiguous structured spectrogram reads, chunk 32 reached 218,670
samples/second compared with 179,964 samples/second for chunk 1. This shows a
real access-pattern tradeoff: one-sample chunks favor shuffled training, while
larger chunks can favor sequential or bulk access.

IQ samples were less sensitive to chunk size. Native contiguous reads were
still substantially faster than the current per-sample shuffled path.

Compression and storage
-----------------------

LZF achieved modest savings because the generated random data was not very
compressible:

* Structured IQ: approximately 8.01 MiB uncompressed versus 7.47-7.58 MiB
  compressed.
* Structured spectrogram: approximately 16.01 MiB uncompressed versus
  14.92-14.99 MiB compressed.

The roughly 6-7% reduction generally did not justify the additional latency
for spectrogram data. Uncompressed storage is the stronger provisional default
for model-ready training data. Production data should be measured because its
compressibility may differ.

First-read latency
------------------

Typical first-read latency was approximately:

* Standard: 1.1 ms.
* Packed: 3.6 ms.
* Homogeneous: 1.2-1.6 ms.
* Structured: 1.6 ms.

Packed incurred additional validation and reconstruction work when opened.

Conclusion
----------

For predominantly shuffled model training, the evidence supports uncompressed
structured HDF5 with one-sample chunks, particularly for large samples. For
contiguous bulk access, homogeneous or structured HDF5 with larger chunks can
provide the greatest throughput. Legacy and packed formats remain appropriate
for their compatibility and flexible Signal storage roles but are substantially
slower on the tested model-facing hot paths.

MR10 remains justified. Structured shuffled reads are already the fastest
model-ready path, but shuffled IQ throughput was 45,810 samples/second while
the same uncompressed chunk-8 configuration reached 300,397 samples/second for
contiguous batches. This gap indicates substantial overhead from issuing
shuffled reads one sample at a time and provides the baseline for a batched,
sorted, and coalesced indexed-read implementation.

Limitations
-----------

These are warm-cache raw-reader measurements, not end-to-end training results.
The homogeneous contiguous fast path returns top-level arrays without decoding
metadata, while legacy and packed reads reconstruct complete Signal objects.
Structured reads reconstruct fixed model-facing samples. Results are specific
to the generated workloads, software versions, filesystem, and hardware used
for this run. Production decisions should also consider the confirmed Stage-2
benchmark and measurements from representative production data.
