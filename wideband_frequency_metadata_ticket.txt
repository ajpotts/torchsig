Title: Investigate inconsistent wideband bandwidth and cached frequency-edge metadata

Summary
-------
A completed-metadata snapshot from the default clean wideband pipeline exposed
component bandwidths that exceed the configured range and cached frequency
edges that do not agree with the components' center frequency and bandwidth.
This can make frequency-localization metadata and derived labels incorrect.

Observed configuration
----------------------
- Config: ``wideband_clean_train_all.yaml``
- Sample rate: 10,000,000 Hz
- Configured component bandwidth range: 62,500 to 1,000,000 Hz
- Dataset frequency range: -5,000,000 to 4,999,999 Hz

Observed metadata
-----------------
Two generated 802.11a RTS components reported bandwidths of approximately
6,061,140 Hz and 7,228,571 Hz, substantially above the configured
``bandwidth_max`` of 1,000,000 Hz. A BPSK component reported approximately
1,503,906 Hz, which also exceeded the configured limit.

Some cached frequency edges were inconsistent with the corresponding center
frequency and bandwidth:

1. Component with center frequency -1,969,429.90 Hz and bandwidth
   6,061,140.20 Hz:

   - Expected full-bandwidth edges: approximately
     [-5,000,000.00, 1,061,140.20] Hz
   - Stored edges: approximately
     [-8,929,094.18, 1,061,140.20] Hz

2. Component with center frequency 1,385,713.27 Hz and bandwidth
   7,228,571.47 Hz:

   - Expected full-bandwidth edges: approximately
     [-2,228,572.47, 5,000,000.00] Hz
   - Stored edges: approximately
     [-2,228,572.47, 7,761,661.91] Hz

In both examples one cached edge agrees with the center/bandwidth calculation
while the other does not. This suggests that ``_lower_frequency`` or
``_upper_frequency`` may remain stale after a center-frequency or bandwidth
update. The unexpectedly large bandwidths may be a separate issue involving
protocol-specific generators, bandwidth measurement, clipping, or enforcement
of dataset bandwidth constraints.

Impact
------
- Frequency bounds exposed through signal metadata may be incorrect.
- YOLO and other localization labels derived from these fields may describe an
  invalid or unexpectedly large occupied band.
- Components may extend beyond the configured dataset frequency range.
- Training data may not honor the configured component bandwidth distribution.

Requested investigation
-----------------------
1. Determine where the configured bandwidth is replaced or recalculated for
   protocol-specific and constellation generators.
2. Confirm whether ``bandwidth`` consistently means full two-sided occupied
   bandwidth throughout generation, placement, measurement, and labeling.
3. Audit invalidation/recalculation of ``_lower_frequency`` and
   ``_upper_frequency`` whenever center frequency or bandwidth changes.
4. Verify whether clipping at the dataset frequency limits should modify center
   frequency, bandwidth, cached edges, or all three.
5. Confirm that derived YOLO labels use the final, mutually consistent values.

Acceptance criteria
-------------------
- Final component bandwidths follow the documented configuration semantics, or
  protocol-specific exceptions are explicitly documented and validated.
- ``lower_freq`` and ``upper_freq`` agree with the final ``center_freq`` and
  ``bandwidth`` within an appropriate numerical tolerance.
- Final frequency edges remain within the configured dataset bounds unless an
  explicitly supported behavior says otherwise.
- Cached frequency-derived metadata cannot remain stale after placement or
  bandwidth updates.
- Deterministic tests cover a protocol waveform, a constellation waveform,
  frequency shifting, boundary clipping, and derived localization labels.

Out of scope
------------
The metadata debug logger correctly exposed this state. Changes to signal
generation, bandwidth calculation, frequency placement, or label geometry
should be made in a separate MR from the metadata logging work.
