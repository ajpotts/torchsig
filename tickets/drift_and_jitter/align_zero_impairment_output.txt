Title: Make zero sampling-clock impairment identity-aligned

Summary

Sampling-clock output is currently center-cropped or symmetrically padded to
the input length without accounting for the prototype filter's group delay.
As a result, zero drift and zero jitter do not preserve sample alignment. A
4096-sample tone measured on the current implementation has approximately
-16.25 dB sample-aligned error despite requesting no impairment.

This deterministic fractional shift can dominate the intended ppm-scale
effect and silently misalign sample-indexed annotations.

Proposed change

Derive output alignment from the known prototype-filter group delay, padding,
initial sampling position, and requested initial phase. Crop the output using
those quantities rather than splitting the length difference by parity.

Acceptance criteria

- Zero jitter, zero drift, and zero initial phase preserve input alignment.
- Identity tests cover impulses, tones, random complex IQ, odd and even input
  lengths, and filters with odd and even effective delay.
- Nonzero `initial_phase` produces the documented delay and no additional
  alignment artifact.
- Python and Numba paths retain identical alignment.
- Sample-indexed annotations remain aligned according to the documented
  transform contract.
