Title: Make adjacent-channel interference center-frequency units consistent

Summary

`adjacent_channel_interference` documents `center_frequency` as normalized
relative to the sample rate. Its implementation constructs time as
`arange(n) / sample_rate` and uses `center_frequency` directly in a complex
exponential. That calculation treats `center_frequency` as a physical frequency
in the same units as `sample_rate`, not as a normalized fraction of the sample
rate.

For example, with `sample_rate=4` and `center_frequency=0.2`, the implementation
creates an offset of 0.05 cycles per sample, or 0.05 times the sample rate, rather
than the documented 0.2 times the sample rate.

Proposed change

Choose one unit convention and apply it throughout the functional API,
`AdjacentChannelInterference` transform, defaults, validation, and docstrings.

Using physical frequency units consistent with `sample_rate` is recommended for
consistency with TorchSIG `center_freq` metadata and other DSP functions. If
backward compatibility requires normalized frequency, compute the exponential
directly from the normalized value and clearly name it accordingly.

Acceptance criteria

- The parameter's units are unambiguous in both functional and transform
  documentation.
- The implementation and parameter validation use the documented convention.
- A deterministic tone or FFT-based test verifies the generated interference
  peak at the requested offset.
- Tests use a sample rate other than one so physical and normalized units cannot
  accidentally produce the same expected result.
- Any compatibility impact or migration requirement is documented.
