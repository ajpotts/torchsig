Improve OFDM Windowing for Spectral Suppression and Signal Fidelity

Summary
The current OFDM windowing implementation restores the historical TorchSig 0.6.1 win_start behavior. This is intentional and should remain the scope of the existing MR.
As a follow-up, investigate improvements to OFDM windowing that provide better out-of-band spectral suppression while preserving signal fidelity and compatibility with OFDM demodulation.
Motivation
The restored implementation appends a cyclic suffix, applies complementary halves of a Blackman window to the cyclic prefix and suffix, and overlap-adds adjacent symbols.
Although this smooths symbol transitions, several aspects deserve further investigation:


        
      Overlap-add normalization: The two Blackman window halves do not sum to exactly one, potentially introducing amplitude variation in overlap regions.

        
      Spectral performance: The current regression tests validate waveform construction but do not quantify out-of-band emission reduction.

        
      Cyclic-prefix protection: Windowing modifies samples within the cyclic prefix, potentially reducing the effective guard interval available for multipath protection.

        
      Boundary handling: The first cyclic prefix is tapered while the final cyclic suffix is discarded, producing asymmetric treatment of the beginning and end of a generated waveform.

Proposed Work


        
      Evaluate the existing Blackman windowing implementation against alternative window functions and overlap-add strategies, including windows satisfying the constant-overlap-add (COLA) condition.

        
      Quantify spectral leakage and adjacent-channel power for windowed and unwindowed OFDM signals.

        
      Evaluate amplitude continuity and power variation at symbol boundaries.

        
      Investigate the effect of windowing on OFDM demodulation performance, particularly under multipath conditions.

        
      Review initial and final symbol boundary handling.

        
      Determine whether window type and transition length should be configurable, while retaining the historical behavior as a compatibility option.
