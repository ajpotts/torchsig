Title: Correct frequency coordinates used for wideband overlap detection

Summary

Wideband overlap detection maps each component signal into a time-frequency
rectangle. The frequency edges in `_map_to_coordinates` currently use
`center_freq +/- bandwidth`, even though TorchSIG defines bandwidth as the full
two-sided bandwidth and derives signal edges as
`center_freq +/- bandwidth / 2`.

The frequency normalization also divides by `sample_rate / 2` before scaling by
`fft_size`. Mapping the Nyquist interval `[-sample_rate / 2, sample_rate / 2]`
to FFT bins `[0, fft_size]` requires division by `sample_rate`.

Impact

The resulting rectangles are wider than the component signals and their bin
coordinates can extend to approximately twice the FFT size. When co-channel
overlap is disabled or probabilistic, signals that do not overlap can be
incorrectly rejected.

Proposed change

- Calculate the lower and upper edges using the canonical `lower_freq` and
  `upper_freq` properties, or explicitly use `center_freq +/- bandwidth / 2`.
- Normalize an edge with `(edge + sample_rate / 2) / sample_rate`.
- Keep the existing time-coordinate behavior unless separate testing identifies
  a time-mapping problem.

Acceptance criteria

- A signal spanning the complete Nyquist interval maps to bins 0 through
  `fft_size`.
- A zero-centered signal with bandwidth `sample_rate / 2` maps to bins
  `fft_size / 4` through `3 * fft_size / 4`, subject to rounding.
- Signals whose canonical frequency intervals do not intersect are not marked
  as overlapping solely because of inflated frequency rectangles.
- Signals whose time and canonical frequency intervals intersect are marked as
  overlapping.
- Focused tests cover positive, negative, and zero center frequencies and the
  Nyquist boundaries.
