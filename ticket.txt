Summary

Several TorchSig DSP and impairment functions remain relatively expensive in the current benchmark suite. Investigate whether selected implementations can be accelerated using Numba, particularly functions that contain element-wise numerical loops or repeated sample-by-sample calculations that are not already efficiently vectorized.
The goal is to improve dataset generation and augmentation throughput without changing numerical behavior, output types, reproducibility, or supported platforms.
Motivation

Recent Linux benchmark results show several functions with substantially higher execution times than the simpler transforms.
Potential candidates include:


        
      
doppler: approximately 1.93 ms mean

        
      
spurs: approximately 658 µs mean

        
      
fading: approximately 461 µs mean

        
      
time_varying_noise: approximately 346 µs mean

        
      
quantize: approximately 328 µs mean

        
      
nonlinear_amplifier: approximately 106 µs mean

        
      
intermodulation_products: approximately 84 µs mean

        
      
digital_agc: approximately 17 µs mean

The benchmark results also show that several FFT-heavy operations, including spectrogram generation and passband ripple, are relatively expensive. These may be poor candidates for Numba if most of their runtime is spent inside NumPy or SciPy FFT routines, but they should be profiled to confirm where time is being spent.
Proposed work



        
      Profile the slower benchmarked functions and identify time spent in:


        
      Python loops

        
      scalar calculations

        
      array allocation

        
      NumPy or SciPy library calls

        
      random-number generation



        
      Select functions where Numba is likely to provide a meaningful improvement.

        
      Extract performance-sensitive kernels into small functions compatible with numba.njit.

        
      Preserve a non-Numba implementation or fallback for environments where Numba is unavailable.

        
      Add or extend benchmarks to compare the original and accelerated implementations across representative input sizes.

        
      Verify that JIT compilation time is excluded from steady-state benchmark measurements.

Initial candidates

Prioritize functions with explicit Python loops or sequential state:


        
      digital AGC

        
      fading kernels

        
      nonlinear amplifier

        
      quantization

        
      spur generation

        
      Doppler-related sample processing

Lower-priority candidates include functions dominated by optimized NumPy, SciPy, or FFT operations.
