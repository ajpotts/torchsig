Title: Add deterministic configurable RF/channel augmentation to IQ generation

Problem
Generated IQ records cannot currently opt into explicit frequency offset, phase noise, fading, and noise with reproducible per-record provenance. Reader-side augmentation would make stored data nondeterministic and couple modeling policy to I/O.

Scope
- Add a reusable declarative generation-time RF/channel impairment pipeline.
- Support independent frequency offset (Hz), Gaussian phase noise (degrees), Rayleigh fading (normalized coherence bandwidth and power-delay profile), and AWGN (absolute dB).
- Support optional unit-power normalization before or after impairments.
- Derive random draws from a global seed, stable record identity, and effect name.
- Store actual sampled values, effect order, seed, identity, sample rate, and configuration in signal metadata.
- Keep file readers unchanged and make a disabled configuration a no-op.

Acceptance criteria
- Each impairment works independently and in representative combinations.
- Identical seed, configuration, and record identity produce identical samples and metadata regardless of scheduling or worker count.
- Enabling another effect does not change an already-enabled effect's sampled parameter.
- Transform order is frequency offset, phase noise, fading, AWGN, with normalization explicitly before or after that sequence.
- Invalid configuration is rejected before record generation; record-dependent Nyquist violations are rejected before signal mutation.
- Deterministic tests cover effect behavior, combinations, provenance, no-op behavior, and validation.

Out of scope
- Reader augmentation, seeking, caching, and sidecar parsing.
- Training-time augmentation policy.
- New fading models beyond the existing Rayleigh implementation.
